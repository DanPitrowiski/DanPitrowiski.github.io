Case studies / Wootric

CXInsights: AI feedback analysis that took Wootric from $1M to $5M ARR

Senior Product Designer · Wootric, acquired by InMoment · 2015–2025

What is it?

Wootric started as a survey company. Any business could drop a snippet of code into its website, mobile app, text or email and start collecting NPS, CSAT, or CES responses in minutes.

CXInsights (CXI) is the enterprise layer built on top of it. It pulls feedback from any survey source into one place. It let teams build their own dashboards, and use AI to automatically sort open-ended feedback into themes with sentiment, so a CX team can see what customers are saying at a scale no person could read.

Problems

(1) Too Many Responses to Understand at Scale

Enterprise customers sometimes received millions of responses a month. Companies like DocuSign, Zoom, and Glassdoor had teams exporting spreadsheets of their feedback and building their own dashboards by hand.

(2) Big Bad Data

Customer data sent with surveys was disorganized. For example, one source might pass an empty property called company_name while another source passed organization, account_name, or company. Users would often filter on these and see a blank page.

“It’s so much easier when you have all of the feedback come into one place. You can look at the snapshot view. You can filter. You can triage. Honestly, it’s so much better than going through spreadsheets manually. I never want to do that again.”Customer Insights Manager, Hootsuite

Qualitative and quantitative research

UX Case Study

I designed our UX Case Study and ran a dozen interviews with enterprise customers to understand how they reported on feedback today. Those conversations shaped the core feature list: dashboards to share with executives, all feedback from every source in one place, actionable insights, and automatic categorization.

Benchmarks & data

Product analytics set the baseline. For the Standard Dashboard, weekly actives kept rising, few customers reported confusion, and CX managers were screenshotting it straight into their executive reports. I wanted to redesign it, but the data said customers liked it as it was. So we kept it as the baseline design as we designed for enterprises.

Hypothesis & goals

Goals:

  • Out-of-the-box: After feedback is loaded into the account the customer should see immediate value.
  • Source of Truth: All feedback from every source into one place.
  • Code Free Analysis: Let teams build custom dashboards and reports without a BI tool or spreadsheet.
  • Machine Learning Categorization: Categorize feedback automatically so it scales to millions of responses.
  • Actionable: Turn that data into insights with a purpose.

Design Thinking

  • Simple. There are a lot of places a customer can go for data analysis. We needed to give them value from day 1. An out-of-the-box experience.
  • Actionable. Don't just show. Tell the customer what they can act on to create value.
  • Transparent AI Decision-Making. Every machine-applied tag should say how it was applied and why, right where it appears.
  • Meet people where they report. CX managers live in executive decks, so dashboards had to be clean enough to screenshot.
  • Big Dirty Data. Customers were going to send us unstructured data from all sources. Inconsistent schemas, missing metadata, mislabeled, etc. The user experience needed to work around this.

Competitive analysis

  • Tableau: Very technical analysis BI tool that is great. But customers would need an in-house person to do the analysis for them.
  • Spreadsheets: Manual. Time consuming. But a spreadsheet was the most common answer for how customers handle their feedback now.
  • Smaller AI companies: At the time there were smaller AI companies willing to build custom models for each customer. These were expensive and didn't have the industry knowledge.

Experiments

Categorizing Feedback with Tags

To categorize feedback at scale we needed tags. Some ideas made it to launch, others didn't.

  • Smart Tags: These tags would match automatically using machine learning trained on large sets of feedback. For each industry, we developed a set of tags after working closely with customers. See article here.
  • Deleting Smart Tags and Training the Model: match automatically using machine learning trained on large sets of feedback.
  • Text Match Tags: This let users add specific phrases, such as "credit card" for a BILLING tag.
  • Text Match Tags with Filters: This was the idea of users setting custom filters, and only tagging the feedback that matched. After scoping this with engineering, it would need to launch later.
  • Manual Tags: Manually applied tags.
  • Sentiment Only Tags: This would have been tags only looking for positive, neutral, or negative responses. I argued sentiment should live outside of tagging. In the future, a user could create these on their own.

Survey Feedback Cards

Feedback cards went through the most iterations. We defined a few of the most important features:

  • Readability: The survey feedback should be easy to read and scan through.
  • Clear delineations: Because all surveys were mixed together the feedback needed to look different where it mattered. Score. NPS, CSAT, CES, open-ended only. Positive Sentiment, Neutral, or Negative. etc.
  • Fast Manual Tagging: Tags needed to be easy to add, easy to delete, and easy to understand why a Tag was added in the first place.

Unique Reporting

Some reporting ideas were just too ambitious.

Rapid prototyping

  • AI Controls: Our ML models allowed additional configuration. But in testing it was too confusing. For example our standard was to assign feedback a "Negative" sentiment if the model returned a score between 0-0.25. That range could be adjusted. It resulted in users noticing edge cases where they wanted to adjust the entire model if 2 responses in 100 were wrong. Instead we added the ability to change a Sentiment for one feedback at a time.
  • Custom Reports: The first reports we built worked directly with customers to reproduce the most common reports they made for themselves.
  • Onboarding and Empty-State: I worked directly with our design intern to create an onboarding flow. She did a fantastic job. But we pivoted after talking with our account managers. They planned on personally onboarding Enterprise customers and already preloading their data. So Enterprise customers would never even see the onboarding we were building for them.

Sequencing & scaling out

We built in layers, each one getting tested with customers:

  1. Feedback Breakdown. We needed the view to see all the feedback first.
  2. AI/ML auto-categorization. Smart, Text Match, and Manual tags with sentiment, so the data tells a story.
  3. Enterprise Executive Dashboard. Custom reports and dashboards once customers could see their feedback.
  4. Watchlists. Actionable watchlists from segments customers made themselves.

Outcome

For Enterprise customers, CXInsights became their most used app. Of Enterprise users, 80% of their time was now spent on Feedback Breakdown. The Feedback Breakdown let customers see their data before acting: which segments existed, how much data each held, and a one-click path to dig in or create a report.

Alongside Feedback Breakdown, CXInsights helped carry Wootric from focusing on startups to enterprise. It let us sign our biggest contracts ever and took ARR from $1M to $5M before acquisition by InMoment in January 2021.

Conclusion

The most valuable fix wasn't a new chart. It was admitting that customer data was big, messy, and hard to work with. We needed to design for it. That lesson still applies across the industry: most CX dashboards still deal with empty screens, bad data, and lost trust.

With how AI has taken over, I'm glad I was there on the ground floor in 2020.
