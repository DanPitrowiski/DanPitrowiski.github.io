My Portfolio Was 10 Years Old. AI Helped Me Rebuild It.

What AI did well, where it sucked, and what it means.

October 2026

My portfolio was looking old. No one needed to say it. It was the fact that after a dozen interviews no one mentioned my portfolio. If it wasn't grabbing your attention it wasn't working.

Choosing a direction

Why the Wikipedia style won out over the others. Give Alex Lakas credit by name and link his site.
  • Out of all those portfolios, I kept coming back to the Wikipedia-style site of Alex Lakas.
  • Why it fit me: a long career reads like a biography, and the format is dense, scannable, and familiar to everyone.
  • Citations make claims checkable. Acquisitions, customers, and press link to their sources, which builds trust with a hiring manager.
  • The infobox gives the quick facts at a glance: role, timeline, team, customers.

Building the system

How the work split between you and AI. Keep it concrete: you described the outcome and judged the result, and Claude wrote the HTML and CSS. Pick one or two moments where you pushed back.
  • One shared stylesheet for every page: color and type tokens, light and dark mode, the masthead, Contents, and the infobox.
  • A second stylesheet for case studies: stat rows, pull quotes, screenshot pairs, and a "To write" box that keeps half-finished sections visible.
  • A lightbox, so any screenshot can be opened full size.
  • The case study template came straight out of the research: the common sections became the outline for every case study.
  • Late change: pinning the infobox to the top right so nothing runs underneath it, and a "DJP" home link that follows you down the page.

Digging for evidence

This is the most surprising part of the story, so give it room. The point: AI was most useful as a research assistant, not as a writer.

A 2013 inbox

  • My Enthuse work was over a decade old, and most of it was gone from the web.
  • I exported my old work email and had Claude sort through it for designs, launch numbers, and the stories behind them.
  • That turned up six design stories, the launch-week sign-up numbers, fliers, video boards, and photos from the championships.

Customer voices from before the acquisition

  • Wootric's G2 listing was renamed InMoment after the acquisition, so I used only reviews from before January 2021 as testimonials.
  • G2 blocked automated browsing, so I copied the reviews over myself. Not every step can be automated.

Press that survived

  • We found 2012–2013 announcements still live on the America East, CSU Monterey Bay, and Miami Hurricanes sites, plus TechCrunch.
  • It also caught a mix-up: a Forbes article about a similar app, and an unrelated UK company also called Enthuse.
  • A date check showed one partnership was announced before the launch I'd called the "first," so I reworded it.

Where I stayed in charge

The section hiring managers care about most. Every choice here is a design decision AI couldn't make for you. Keep the list tight and specific.
  • Positioning. I rewrote Wootric and Press Ganey so they talk about design, not product management.
  • Story order. I reordered the six Enthuse stories so the strongest design work leads.
  • Editing. I kept the infobox short, even when that meant leaving a partner off the list.
  • Crops and images. I cropped photos so the moment shows (a fan's face and the ball on a first pitch), and chose a phone screen for the thumbnail.
  • Layout. I tried the pinned infobox as an experiment, compared every page, then decided to keep it.

What went wrong

Honest and a little funny. Readers trust a write-up more when it includes the mistakes.
  • The lightbox jumped to the bottom of the page. The cause was one missing curly brace in the CSS.
  • I accidentally overwrote the homepage and lost a round of changes. We rebuilt it from a backup. Lesson: commit to git after every round.
  • Some sites block automated tools (G2 reviews, Medium images), so those steps went back to me.
  • AI drafts read smooth and generic until I put my own voice back in.

What I'd tell other designers

Three to five lessons, then a closing line that ties back to the hook. End with a link to the portfolio itself.
  • Do the research before the prompt. A strong reference gets you further than a blank page.
  • Treat AI like a fast production team: you set the direction and review every result.
  • Use it to dig. The best material was buried in old email, old reviews, and old press.
  • Commit often, and keep a backup before every big change.
  • AI can build the page. It can't decide what your story is.
